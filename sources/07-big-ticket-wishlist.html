The dreams that decluttering reminds you of. Park them here, plan them kindly.

How to use this page

While spring cleaning you'll spot the things that cleaning can't fix — sagging mattress, blinds near death, that one reliable-but-ugly chair. Write them here instead of buying mid-sweep. Then let the sheet sit: items that still seem worth it in a month are the real list. Earmark a small monthly amount if a line item keeps calling to you.

The wish list

Prioritize with three passes

  1. Safety & function first — leaking, wiring, mold or damp, failing things that damage the house. These outrank everything pretty.
  2. Daily-drag fixers — the item that annoys you every single day wins over the item that would impress twice a year.
  3. Joy purchases — guilt-free. A home that sparks warmth is a real goal; this pass exists precisely so you can say yes to one lovely thing.

The 30-day hold

Enter the date in “Wanted since.” If a month passes and you still want it, price it properly (new, used, sale) and put the first dollar aside. Wanting something through a whole laundry cycle and a bad week is a decent proxy for actually wanting it.

Tips before buying big

  • Check if it's repairableA $40 fix on a $400 habit item often outruns a $400 replace.
  • Measure twice, including doorways & stairs
  • Check outlet/valve/hookup compatibility first
  • Sell the old one on the same day it leaves — drama-free
  • Does it need a maintenance cal? Add small annual item here instead of a wish
  • Free options first: freecycle, marketplace, curb alerts, hand-me-downs
  • Budget line item gets a name (“New Curtain Fund”); named money moves
